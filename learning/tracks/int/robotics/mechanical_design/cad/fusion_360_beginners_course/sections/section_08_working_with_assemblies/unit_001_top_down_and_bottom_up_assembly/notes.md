# Notes: Assemblies, Top-Down vs Bottom-Up
 
**Status:** Jumped ahead (banked XP). Learned early to assemble my digital twin.
 
## Bottom-up (traditional)
- Each part is designed in its **own file**, then inserted into an assembly and put together there.
- Best for **off-the-shelf parts** (motors, electronics, screws, props).
- Parts are edited in their own file; the assembly picks up the change.
- Parts are reusable across assemblies.
## Top-down
- Parts are **designed inside the assembly**, using existing components as a reference to build off.
- Best for **new custom parts** that have to fit around others (frames, mounts, brackets).
- Parts are created and edited in the assembly itself.
- Referenced parts stay linked, so changes carry through.
- Downside: dependencies can break if a referenced feature is changed or deleted.
