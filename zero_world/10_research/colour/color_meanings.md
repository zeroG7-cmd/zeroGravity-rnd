# Color Meanings — Research

*zero_world/10_research/color_meanings.md*

Sourced research behind zer0Gravity's color palette. This file holds the "why," cited. For how these colors get applied across the brand, see `09_design_language/color_system.md`. For the personal origin of why this research mattered in the first place, see `01_spirit/color_origin.md`.

## Black `#000000`
Void, nothingness before the spark. In Pan-African symbolism, tied to the flag Marcus Garvey designed in 1920, black represents unity and reclaimed pride, "Black is Beautiful."
Source: [Colors of Black History Month](https://www.colorpsychology.org/blog/colors-of-black-history-month-the-symbolism-of-colors-in-cultural-movements/)

## Gold `#C9A227`
Among the Akan people of Ghana, gold was considered a physical manifestation of the sun and the supreme creator god. Because gold doesn't corrode, it was tied to spiritual purity and immortality. It also marked kingship, political power, and cultural identity.
Source: [Akan Goldweights](https://momaa.org/akan-goldweights/)

## Purple `#7B27C0`
Rare and valuable, made from scarce natural dyes, which restricted it to nobility. Among the Asante, Kente cloth with purple patterns was reserved specifically for royalty and sacred ceremony. Among the Yoruba, purple marked regal attire and divine connection.
Source: [Why Purple Is a Royal Color in Africa](https://medium.com/@nellieoak/why-purple-is-a-royal-color-in-africa-a9540cdc068f)

## Wine `#6B1E42`
No independent African tradition of its own found in research, but it sits directly between two that do: purple's reserved, sacred royalty, and red's meaning within Pan-African symbolism, sacrifice, resilience, the fight for justice. Read as a bridge color between the two.

## Green `#0D7A54`
Hope, growth, and renewal, and a symbol of Africa's natural resources. From the Pan-African flag.
Source: [Colors of Black History Month](https://www.colorpsychology.org/blog/colors-of-black-history-month-the-symbolism-of-colors-in-cultural-movements/)

## Navy / Indigo `#3B3760`
Indigo cloth marked wealth, abundance, and fertility across West Africa, from Senegal to Cameroon. Among the Yoruba, the dyeing process was a spiritual practice, honoring the deity Iya Mapo. Before synthetic dye, the skill of turning plain white cotton into deep blue cloth was mysterious and highly valuable, passed down generation to generation, and it elevated dyers to esteemed status.
Source: [Indigo in West Africa](https://www.adireafricantextiles.com/textiles-resources-sub-saharan-africa/some-major-west-african-textile-traditions/indigo-in-west-africa/)

## Terracotta / Rust `#A6332A`
Nok terracotta sculptures are the oldest known figurative sculpture south of the Sahara, made from local clay and gravel, individually hand-shaped rather than mass-produced. Foundational to African art history, and still not fully understood by scholars.
Source: [Nok Terracottas, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nok-terracottas-500-b-c-200-a-d)

## Beige `#D6C4A4`
From bogolanfini, Malian mud cloth. The earth tones represent connection to land, stability, and respect for the earth. The pigment is derived directly from the mud itself, not an added dye, the color is the earth.
Source: [The Art of African Mud Cloth (Bogolanfini)](https://www.momentslog.com/culture/the-art-of-african-mud-cloth-bogolanfini-dyeing-techniques-and-symbolism)

## Mauve `#A85891`
No confirmed African historical lineage found. Its documented history is Victorian English, an accidentally invented synthetic dye. Included in the palette as a personal and aesthetic choice, this is your sister's pick, not a historical one. Worth being honest about that distinction rather than forcing a meaning that isn't there.
