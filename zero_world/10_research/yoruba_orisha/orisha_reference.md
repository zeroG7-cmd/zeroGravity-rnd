# Zero's World — Power System Reference

## How to Start This Properly

Worldbuilding falls apart when everything lives in your head — write it down
even in rough form, especially the *rules*, since consistency is what makes
a power system feel earned rather than arbitrary.

**Suggested folder structure**, separate from your technical repos (this is
a creative project, not code):

```
zero-universe/
  01_power_system/
    orisha_reference.md       <- this document
    system_rules.md           <- the actual mechanics/limits (see below)
    zero_ogun.md               <- Zero's specific character notes
  02_characters/
    zero.md
    (one file per major character, as they exist)
  03_setting/
    nigeria_future.md          <- the speculative future setting
    england_ties.md            <- Zero's origin/connection there
    timeline.md
  04_events/
    (one file per major story event, once you have them)
```

**The single most valuable thing to write first**: not more lore, but the
actual *rules* of the power system — specifically, what it costs. You
already have the "personality resonance" mechanic (a person's nature
determines which Orisha's essence they can manifest). The next real
question, the one that gives it Hunter x Hunter-level weight rather than
Dragon Ball flash: **what does using the power cost the user?** Real,
consistently-applied costs are what separate a sophisticated system from
raw power fantasy. Worth sitting with this before writing any events.

## Orisha Reference

Grounded in real research, not invented — the Orishas are a living
spiritual tradition, not dead mythology, so accuracy matters here more
than in typical fantasy worldbuilding.

### Eshu (Elegua, Elegba)
**Domain**: Crossroads, communication, choice, spiritual pathways
**Personality**: Trickster, embodies duality, guardian of paths — can open
or close them
**Story potential**: A character who deals in information, choices,
consequences of decisions not yet made

### Obatala
**Domain**: Creation, wisdom, purity — creator of humankind in some
traditions
**Personality**: Clarity, justice, peace; regarded as king among the
Orishas
**Story potential**: A moral center, a judge, someone whose power is
tied to seeing clearly rather than acting first

### Orunmila
**Domain**: Divination, destiny, foresight — guardian of the Ifá oracle
**Personality**: Wisdom, patience
**Story potential**: Prophecy, foresight-based abilities, the cost of
knowing what's coming

### Shango (Sango)
**Domain**: Thunder, lightning, fire
**Personality**: Bold, commanding, passionate, a warrior-king; associated
with drumming and dance
**Story potential**: Direct confrontation, leadership through force of
presence

### Ogun — *Zero's Orisha*
**Domain**: Iron, war, technology, labor, agriculture — the smith, the
opener of roads
**Personality**: Direct, gruff, unglamorous about effort; protector of
laborers
**Story potential**: See dedicated section below

### Yemoja (Yemaya)
**Domain**: Ocean, motherhood — mother of all waters
**Personality**: Nurturing, protective, emotionally deep
**Story potential**: A guardian figure, someone whose strength is
protective rather than aggressive

### Oshun (Osun)
**Domain**: Rivers, love, beauty, fertility
**Personality**: Sweetness, attraction — can tip into vanity
**Story potential**: Diplomacy through charm rather than force, healing

### Oya
**Domain**: Wind, storms, transformation — guardian of the dead and the
marketplace
**Personality**: Change, rebirth, upheaval
**Story potential**: A character who forces change on stagnant
situations, possibly at real cost to relationships/stability

*(Others exist and are worth researching as the cast grows — Osanyin,
herbs and healing; Obaluaiye/Shopona, disease and healing; Oba,
loyalty and sacrifice.)*

**A real, documented practice worth building the system's actual
mechanic around**: in genuine Orisha worship, a devotee "mounted" by an
Orisha visibly takes on that Orisha's personality — Oshun's devotees
become flirtatious, Shango's become commanding, Ogun's become blunt and
direct. This is the real-world root of "your personality determines
which Orisha resonates through you" — not an invented rule, an observed
one.

## Zero and Ogun — Why the Connection Works

This isn't a symbol borrowed from outside the story — it's the same
impulse Zero already has in real life, wearing fiction's clothes.

**The forge, literally and philosophically.** Ogun is the smith who
takes shapeless raw iron and, through effort and heat, turns it into
something with purpose — a tool, a blade, a road cleared through what
was previously impassable. Zero's own stated drive — *taking infinite
potential and forging it into a one* — isn't a metaphor applied to
Ogun's mythology from outside. It's the same shape, arrived at
independently. That's what makes it feel earned rather than assigned.

**The EMS shield drone as Ogun's signature act.** Ogun protects by
making — not by standing in front of danger himself, but by building
the thing that stands there instead. A drone that shields people from
harmful projectiles is a direct, modern expression of exactly what
Ogun does in the old stories: forge protection out of raw material.
This can be treated as Zero's signature creation, or the first of
several — worth deciding once more of the cast exists.

**Ogun the road-opener, not just the warrior.** Ogun's mythology isn't
only about the forge and iron — he's also the one who clears roads,
literally cutting paths through what blocks passage. Zero's instinct
toward resolving conflict through talking rather than fighting (the
Talk no Jutsu instinct) isn't a separate trait bolted onto the Ogun
concept — it's the same nature pointed at a different kind of
obstacle. Opening a road through a forest and opening a path through a
stalled argument are the same act, aimed differently. Zero's power
doesn't need two separate modes; forge and diplomacy can be read as one
underlying nature — *take what is chaotic, shape it into something
that can move forward* — applied to metal in one case and to people in
another.

**Absorbing fear, making people feel safe.** Ties directly to the
protector aspect of Ogun (guardian of laborers, the one who clears
danger from the path). Worth deciding as a concrete rule: does this
cost Zero something each time — does absorbed fear have to go
somewhere, does it accumulate, does forging it into calm leave a
residue in him? This is the detail that would give the power real
weight rather than making him simply "the safe one" by default.

**The Nigeria thread and the future setting.** A speculative, hopeful,
technologically advanced future for Nigeria — written on your own
terms, pushing back against how that future usually gets flattened or
dismissed by outside voices — sits within a real, serious storytelling
tradition: Afrofuturism. Zero doesn't need to be the one who unites the
country; the story can be about him being on a conquest to *find* the
person who could, while doing Ogun's real work in the meantime — building,
protecting, opening roads, one person and one drone at a time. The
England tie can function as origin/displacement — someone shaped
partly outside the culture he's ultimately drawn back toward, which is
its own honest, common experience worth writing directly rather than
smoothing over.

## Open Questions Worth Answering Next

1. What does Zero's power cost him, specifically and consistently?
2. Is the EMS drone his one signature creation, or one of several?
3. How literally speculative is the Nigeria setting — near-future,
   distant, alternate timeline?
4. Does "resonating" with an Orisha happen once (born with it, fixed)
   or can it shift/deepen based on how a character changes over time?
